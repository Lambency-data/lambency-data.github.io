How far you can see
on public data alone

One piece a week, taking apart one industry's profit and loss with material anyone can open. Audit reports from 38,990 externally audited companies, a full census of 27,908 gyms, seven weeks of Olive Young shelves. We don't count the data as it arrives; we rebuild it against the original documents before writing a number. We also mark where public data goes blind. Past that point only a company's own data can measure it, and that is our work.

Written by Caff, a data scientist of ten years. I led a data hub covering 1.95 million customers, and built and experimentally validated CRM models across four countries with a global eyewear brand. I also built an intersection-risk model for the New York City Council's Vision Zero, two R packages on CRAN, and the open-source bytemescan and lambency_rfm.

The list price was honest. That turned out to be the harder problem

Seven weeks of Olive Young shelf prices. The usual suspicion — that list prices are padded to manufacture a discount — did not survive: they held steady for 96.2% of products and matched the undiscounted week's real selling price to the won 97.2% of the time. 15 E-commerce 2026.10

Raise It to Stand Still — E-commerce Advertising Is Not a Growth Cost

Ad spend and sales, two years apiece, pulled from the audit reports of 71 e-commerce companies among Korea's 38,990 audited firms. Companies that raised ad spend by more than 20% grew a median +0.9% in e-commerce and +15.8% in cosmetics; those that cut, −17.8% and −5.4%. The sensitivity to advertising cannot be told apart from cosmetics — the curve simply sits eleven points lower. In this industry advertising is a maintenance cost.

16 Cash flow 2026.10

100 of Profit on Paper, 72 in the Bank — And Which Company Changes Every Year

Book profit next to cash in the bank for 348 profitable Korean consumer companies in 5 industries, from their audit reports. Above a 12% margin, a company that writes 100 of profit in the books receives 72 in the bank. Which company does it changes every year (two-year correlation −0.15). What makes it is not growth but advertising and other people's channels: spend over 10% of sales on ads and sell through others' channels, and 44% see cash leave the bank in a profitable year.

Rules of the series

Only figures verified against the original source. Secondary aggregates and press summaries are never used as headline numbers, and no comparison is drawn without stating standalone or consolidated basis.

The limits come first. Public sources cannot establish incrementality, contribution profit by channel, or repeat purchase. What follows is a hypothesis, not a measurement.

No sector is being written off. These industries were broadly having a good year. What diverged was execution, company by company.

Lambency uses public data to diagnose where to look, and internal data to test whether it holds.
